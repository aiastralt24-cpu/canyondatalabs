## 1. Executive Summary

Canyon needs a premium, international-grade web presence that positions the company as a deep-tech studio for enterprise AI, data engineering, custom enterprise systems, and strategic AI consulting. The website must not feel like a generic AI agency landing page. It should feel restrained, intelligent, technical, and long-horizon.

The current HTML establishes a strong creative direction: a dark canyon-inspired visual system, slow geological motion, a single-page descent narrative, and a brand promise anchored by the line "Some problems run deeper than software." This PRD formalizes that direction into a complete product, content, design, technical, analytics, accessibility, SEO, and launch specification.

The website's job is not only to explain services. Its job is to create conviction. Enterprise buyers should feel that Canyon understands complex systems. Investors should see a company with a durable product path. Talent should sense the seriousness of the work. The site must serve all three audiences without diluting the brand.

## 2. Product Vision

Build a digital brand platform that makes Canyon feel like the inevitable partner for deep, enterprise-grade intelligence systems.

The site should communicate:

- Canyon is a deep-tech studio, not a vendor.
- Canyon Data Labs is the services and engineering face.
- Canyon AI is the product and model face.
- Canyon builds intelligence infrastructure that compounds over years.
- The brand is premium because it is disciplined, not decorative.

The ideal visitor reaction:

1. "What is this?" - curiosity.
2. "These people are serious." - credibility.
3. "I want to talk to them." - desire.

## 3. Strategic Context

### 3.1 Brand Foundation

From the brand book, Canyon is built around three core metaphors:

- Depth: solving problems that do not have shallow answers.
- Time: building systems that outlast hype cycles.
- Force: data as the river, intelligence as the current, Canyon as the terrain that turns flow into decision.

The website must preserve these ideas throughout content, motion, structure, and interaction.

### 3.2 Market Position

Canyon sits between elite consulting and product engineering:

- More strategic than a software development vendor.
- More implementation-capable than a consulting firm.
- More bespoke than a SaaS product.
- More durable than an AI demo shop.

### 3.3 Competitive Differentiation

Canyon should avoid common AI market patterns:

- No excessive buzzwords.
- No template-blue enterprise SaaS design.
- No shallow claims about "unlocking AI."
- No overstuffed feature grids.
- No startup hype language without proof.

Differentiation should come from:

- Strategic seriousness.
- Beautiful restraint.
- Specific service architecture.
- Evidence of engineering depth.
- A clear two-face brand model: services now, products over time.

## 4. Business Objectives

### 4.1 Primary Objectives

- Generate qualified enterprise conversations for Canyon Data Labs.
- Establish Canyon AI as a credible future-facing product identity.
- Build trust with investors, partners, and senior talent.

- Improve discoverability for enterprise AI, data engineering, and custom software keywords.
- Provide a polished destination for outbound sales, founder introductions, hiring, and investor conversations.

## 9. Functional Requirements

### 9.1 Navigation

Requirement:

- The navigation must remain fixed at the top of the viewport.
- It must visually compress on scroll.
- Desktop navigation must show section links and primary CTA.
- Mobile navigation must provide access to all primary sections.

Current gap:

- Mobile currently hides links without providing an alternate menu.

Acceptance criteria:

- On desktop, all nav links scroll smoothly to their sections.
- On mobile, users can access all navigation destinations.
- Navigation does not cover section headings after anchor scroll.
- Nav contrast remains readable over all backgrounds.

Priority: P0

### 9.2 Hero

Requirement:

- The hero must establish Canyon as a premium enterprise AI brand in the first viewport.
- The animated canyon canvas must load reliably and never produce a blank first screen.
- The hero must include brand name, category signal, tagline, and supporting statement.

Current implementation:

- Canvas geological strata animation.
- Hero text: "CANYON."
- Tagline: "Some problems run deeper than software."
- Supporting text: "We build the deep intelligence enterprises can't buy off the shelf."

Acceptance criteria:

- Hero renders in under 2 seconds on fast 4G.
- Canvas animation degrades to a static visual when reduced motion is enabled.
- Hero text remains readable at 320px, 390px, 768px, 1440px, and 1920px widths.
- There is always visible content even if external animation libraries fail.

Priority: P0

### 9.3 Manifesto Section

Requirement:

- Explain Canyon's founding idea in a way that is emotional but precise.
- Include Depth, Time, and Force as the three conceptual pillars.

Acceptance criteria:

- Each pillar is understandable without internal context.
- Copy remains short and high-signal.
- Section creates a clear transition from brand feeling to business seriousness.

Priority: P0

### 9.4 Capability Depths

Requirement:

- Present Canyon's services as layered depth, not a generic services grid.

Required layers:

- Surface Layer: Data Engineering and Infrastructure.
- Mid Layer: AI and Model Development.
- Deep Layer: Custom Enterprise Software.
- The Core: Strategic AI Consulting.

Acceptance criteria:

- Each layer includes name, capability headline, descriptive outcome, and capability tags.
- Layout supports comparison across layers.
- Copy explains outcomes, not only activities.
- Each layer can later link to a detail page without changing the section architecture.

Priority: P0

### 9.5 Two Faces Section

Requirement:

- Explain the relationship between Canyon Data Labs and Canyon AI.

Acceptance criteria:

- Canyon Data Labs is understood as the services and engineering face.
- Canyon AI is understood as the product and model face.
- Both feel part of one brand system.
- Users can follow CTAs for project inquiry and product roadmap interest.

Priority: P0

### 9.6 Proof / Numbers Section

Requirement:

- Provide concise proof signals without inventing unsupported metrics.

Current proof points:

- 100% bespoke.

- Long-view / infinity.

Acceptance criteria:

- No fake client logos, fake metrics, or unverifiable claims.

### 9.7 Vision Section

Requirement:

Acceptance criteria:

- Copy clearly addresses more than buyers without becoming scattered.
- Section feels like culmination, not another generic CTA.
- The "year one" idea is preserved.

Priority: P0

### 9.8 Contact CTA

V1:

V1.1:

## 11. Design Requirements

### 11.1 Visual Direction

The visual system is "Geological Dusk":

- Rock Black: `#0D0907`.
- Canyon Purple: `#1A0F1E`.
- Canyon Rust: `#C45C26` to `#D4622A`.
- Amber: `#E8893F`.
- Sandstone Bone: `#F0EAE0`.
- Bone Dim: `#B8AEA0`.
- Sand: `#9C8E7B`.
- Ice Blue: `#4FC3F7`.

### 11.2 Color Rules

Follow the 70 / 20 / 8 / 2 discipline:

- 70% rock surfaces.
- 20% bone text.
- 8% rust accent.
- 2% ice signal.

Acceptance criteria:

- Rust is used for primary accent and key moments only.
- Ice Blue appears only for AI/product/data signals.
- Body copy is never set in rust for long passages.
- Contrast meets WCAG AA at minimum.

### 11.3 Typography

Display:

- Fraunces, weights 300-600.
- Used for headlines, signature lines, and emotional emphasis.

Text/UI:

- Inter, weights 300-700.
- Used for body copy, UI, links, and labels.

Technical signature:

- JetBrains Mono.
- Used for labels, metadata, capability tags, and metrics.

Acceptance criteria:

- Display type has strong scale and tight line-height.
- Body text is capped to readable line lengths.
- Labels use uppercase mono styling with tracking.
- No font substitution for the wordmark.

### 11.4 Layout

Requirements:

- 12-column desktop grid.
- 8px spacing base.
- Generous section padding, typically 120px+ on desktop.
- Mobile spacing adapted without losing premium whitespace.
- Near-square corners, around 3px for buttons and 8px or less for cards.

Acceptance criteria:

- The page feels spacious but not empty.
- No nested decorative card stacks.
- Text never overlaps or clips.
- Layout remains stable during animation and font loading.

### 11.5 Motion

Motion principle:

- Motion should feel like erosion: patient, inevitable, smooth.

Required motion:

- Hero strata movement.
- Scroll reveal.
- Nav compression.
- Gentle parallax where useful.
- Marquee only if it does not distract.

Acceptance criteria:

- Supports `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- No bouncy easing.
- No motion that delays content comprehension.
- No blank states while waiting for animation.

## 12. Accessibility Requirements

Target: WCAG 2.2 AA.

Requirements:

- Semantic HTML landmarks: `header`, `nav`, `main`, `section`, `footer`.
- One logical `h1`.
- Sequential heading hierarchy.
- Keyboard-accessible navigation.
- Visible focus states.
- Reduced motion support.
- Sufficient color contrast.
- Accessible names for links and buttons.
- Canvas must be decorative or provide fallback text if meaningful.
- Marquee must be `aria-hidden` if decorative.

Current gaps:

- Navigation should be wrapped in semantic `header`.
- Mobile nav accessibility is incomplete because links are hidden.
- Icon/arrow characters should have accessible labels if used as controls.

Acceptance criteria:

- Lighthouse accessibility score 95+.
- Manual keyboard test passes.
- Reduced motion mode has no moving canvas, marquee, or parallax.
- Screen reader order follows visual order.

## 13. Performance Requirements

Target performance:

- Lighthouse Performance: 90+ on mobile.
- Largest Contentful Paint: under 2.5 seconds.
- Cumulative Layout Shift: under 0.1.
- Interaction to Next Paint: under 200ms.
- JavaScript bundle: keep minimal for a one-page site.

Requirements:

- Avoid blocking render on third-party animation libraries.
- Self-host or optimize fonts where possible.
- Use `font-display: swap` or equivalent.
- Canvas animation must be efficient and pause when page is hidden.
- External scripts should be deferred.
- Respect low-power and reduced-motion environments.

Current risks:

- Google Fonts and CDN scripts introduce network dependency.
- GSAP, ScrollTrigger, and Lenis may be heavy for the current page's needs.
- Canvas animation must be tested on mobile devices.

Acceptance criteria:

- Page remains usable if CDN scripts fail.
- No console errors in production.
- No long tasks over 200ms during initial load.
- Canvas does not drain mobile battery excessively.
